Teaching a sales team to trust an agent that works while they sleep.

Carmen is Airstride’s AI agent for partner acquisition. It sources, qualifies and contacts partners on its own. The automation was never the hard part. Getting a sceptical partnership manager to hand over the relationship was.

01 · Context

Partnerships are high-leverage and painfully manual.

Partnership managers spend most of the week on the work around partnerships: finding companies that fit, vetting them, finding the right person, writing the first message, chasing the follow-up. Weeks pass before one qualified conversation happens.

Airstride’s bet was to hand that whole pipeline to Carmen. My job wasn’t to design a dashboard for it. It was to design the moments where a person decides whether to let go of the wheel.

  1. P.01
    The leap of faith

    Carmen writes in your name to companies you may one day partner with. One tone-deaf message can burn a relationship before it starts.

  2. P.02
    The black box

    An agent working around the clock is invisible by default. If you can’t see what it is doing, you can’t trust it, or stop it.

  3. P.03
    The setup wall

    Configuring an agent usually means a 40-field form. Most people quit before the agent ever runs.

02 · Configure

Setup as a dialogue. Carmen does the research; you react.

Instead of asking people to describe their business, Carmen reads it first and shows back what she understood. The user’s only job is to react: confirm the market, then judge five sample partners one at a time, each with a match score and the reasons Carmen picked it. Every “good match” and “not a fit” teaches the model what good looks like.

After that, configuration stays a conversation. Carmen reports what she found in a chat, and the user steers her in plain language instead of hunting through settings.

Pilot users came in braced for a long form and found the homework already done. The first thing they did was agree. Setup went from days to minutes.
03 · Command

One screen to watch an agent work, and step in.

The command center answers the question every partnership manager asks at 9am: what did Carmen do while I was away, and is it working? Three small decisions did most of the work. None of them are flashy. Trust in an agent comes from being able to see its work, read what it is doing, and stop it without hunting for the button.

04 · Connect

The riskiest handoff: your real LinkedIn account.

Connecting LinkedIn hands Carmen the keys to someone’s professional identity. So the flow does the opposite of most sign-in screens: it slows down and over-explains. The right-hand column always answers the question of the moment: what you’ll need, how to turn on 2FA, and once you’re connected, exactly what Carmen can and cannot do. Credentials are encrypted and never stored, and disconnecting is one click.

Failure got the same care. When verification fails, the error names the three likely causes instead of a generic “something went wrong”, so people fix it themselves instead of opening a ticket.

Naming the limits in plain words, cannot post, cannot touch your password, reassured people more than any security badge would have.

05 · Outcome

The interface, not the model, moved the numbers.

Carmen rolled out to design-partner workspaces through 2025. The model had been capable for months. Usage changed once its work was visible and setup was quick enough that partnership managers stopped checking over its shoulder.

3.4×
More qualified partner conversations per rep each month, against the manual baseline.
71%
Less time on manual sourcing, list-building and first-touch outreach.
18.9%
Average reply rate across Carmen’s automated outreach, above the category norm.
9 min
Median time to configure and launch a live campaign, down from a multi-day setup.
In closing

Carmen turned partner acquisition from weeks of manual sourcing into an agent people actually hand the work to: a nine-minute setup that teaches it what a good partner looks like, a command center that shows its work as it happens, and a LinkedIn handoff that says plainly what it can and cannot do.
