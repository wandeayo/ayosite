01 · Context

UX was showing up on sales calls as a named objection.

Airstride’s platform covered deals, CRM account mapping, partner training, content and engagement. The capability was real. What was failing was the surface: prospects evaluated it, then went with a rival and named the interface as the tipping point.

02 · Problems

Three problems. Only one of them was on screen.

Fixing the screens without addressing the other two would have meant designing everything twice.

  1. P.01
    The deal-loser

    Prospects named the interface as the reason they picked a competitor. UX had become a line item on the sales call.

  2. P.02
    The revision spiral

    Stakeholder churn sent finished screens back for variations. Design time went to decisions that would not stay made.

  3. P.03
    The implementation gap

    Builds drifted from the designs. The Mantine UI library gave developers choices that diverged from intent, with no close review to catch it.

03 · The modules

Each module answers one question at a glance.

I worked module by module, starting from the question a partnership manager brings to it, then designing every state around that answer: empty, first run, in progress, done.

Two decisions carried across every module. Detail opens as an overlay rather than a new page, so people never lose their place in a list. And every flow that touches sensitive data, CRM access or partner permissions, says what it needs before it asks.

In closing

Airstride’s interface went from the reason prospects walked away to something paying customers said they’d recommend. Six modules and a new overview now share one system, each built around the question a partnership manager brings to it, and the marketing site I redesigned alongside it lifted conversion by 84%.
