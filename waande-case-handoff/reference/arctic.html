Redesigning the smart ice bath app, so the tub runs without thinking about the app.

Arctic Edge makes smart cooling tubs. Their companion app worked, but read as cluttered: competing surfaces, decorative colour, and a temperature you had to look at twice. The brief was a redesign people could operate at a glance, often with wet hands.

01 · Context

A hardware app inside a third-party shell.

The app runs inside Tuya’s smart-device platform, so pairing, the account and back-to-list navigation were inherited and not mine to touch. Everything inside the device screen was.

I worked with the product team on scope and screen inventory, and with the hardware team on the operator controls (ozone, deviation, fan speed) the settings needed to expose.

02 · The audit

What the legacy app got wrong.

Reading the old home screen as a UI artefact, two problems outweighed the rest.

  1. P.01
    The temperature was hard to read

    The most important number sat inside a textured 3D ice sphere. The texture fought the digits.

  2. P.02
    There was no system state

    No connection indicator, no cooling or heating badge. The only “off” cue was a button label.

  3. P.03
    Everything weighed the same

    A timing pill, a lock, an OFF circle and a power button all carried equal weight, and the colour was decorative, not functional.

03 · The system

One home screen, three levels of hierarchy.

The temperature sits in display weight inside a ring that carries the state colour. Set Temperature and Session Timer collapse into rows beneath it, and a dock holds schedule, power and lock. Power gets the bright accent because it is the only action that is both primary and immediate.

Colour does work, never decoration: blue for cooling, red for heating, grey when off, green for power and verified states.

04 · Safety surfaces

Every irreversible action wears the same shape.

Lock, connection lost, force start, system off and session resume share one anatomy: a mark that reads as a state, a short title, a one-sentence consequence, and always two actions.

Force Start is the loudest sheet in the app, but the committed button stays neutral white so the visual weight sits on Pause, not on the destructive action. Connection Lost swaps the spinner for a numbered checklist people can actually follow.

05 · Temperature

One dial, two zones.

Set Temperature mirrors the home dial. Below 25°C the gradient sits cold with a snowflake and a blue zone card; above it warms to amber with a wave. The plus, minus, slider and Set Temp action never move.

06 · Schedule and session

Two recurring flows, two surfaces.

Cooling schedules are timer cards with a coloured left edge, green for power on and red for power off, so the list reads as state without parsing. Adding one is five fields on a single sheet, no nesting.

Once a session runs, the dial recedes and a green countdown takes the centre. One line of guidance, two actions, and a summary at the end.

In closing

Arctic Edge went from a cluttered companion app to one home screen that answers the two questions that matter, how cold it is and what state it’s in, at a glance. Around it sits a consistent set of safety sheets, a single dial for temperature, and schedule and session flows that read without thinking.
